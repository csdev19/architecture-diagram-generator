✨ **Feature** — `/editor` is a canvas now. It fills the window, the palette and
the JSON float over it and both close, tiles are dragged in from the palette,
groups are dragged out around them, and the five tools all say what they are
instead of standing in as glyphs.

The change underneath it is smaller than it looks and explains everything else.
`canvas: { w, h }` used to be a field the author wrote, and the renderer drew
into it. That made a wall: a node could be "outside the canvas", so the editor
grew a grey table, a white sheet, clamped drags, a grow-the-sheet rule and a
sheet-size panel — every one of them an apology for the wall. Placing four tiles
outside it clamped all four to the same corner, stacked.

So the frame is derived. `contentFrame` unions the bounds of what the diagram
contains and pads it; `canvas` survives as an optional override for the diagram
that must be an exact size. Coordinates are unbounded, negatives included, and
the margin rule is gone from the schema. In the editor that was a net deletion —
the clamp, the growth rule and the sheet-size panel all went, and nothing
replaced them. Recorded in
[ADR 0002](/architecture/decisions/adr-0002-the-frame-is-derived-not-declared).

`renderSVG` gained an optional frame, and the editor passes the rectangle its
camera is looking at. That is what makes the drawing surface reach every edge of
the window: the paper and the grid are the renderer's, not a CSS backdrop, so
what you look at and what you export come from one function. A test asserts the
two framings produce an identical scene, which is the invariant
[ADR 0001](/architecture/decisions/adr-0001-no-canvas-library-for-the-editor)
turns on.

💄 **UI** — The grid is dashed and neutral rather than solid and blue: covering a
whole window, the old weight read as a mesh laid over the drawing. Paper is now
a choice of five near-whites — `white`, `grey`, `blue`, `cream`, `blush` — named
rather than picked as a hex, like every other colour in the format, and it
exports with the diagram.
