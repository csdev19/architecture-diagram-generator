✨ **Feature** — the relation the format has carried since the document format
landed is now something a person can make.

- **Shift-click** adds to the selection; **⌘G** groups it; **⌘⇧G** gives the
  pieces back exactly where they are.
- A **click selects the outermost group** an element belongs to, which is what
  makes a group read as one object. **Double-click enters it**, so the next
  click reaches inside — the model Figma, tldraw and Excalidraw share, and the
  only way to reach a boundary that frames a group.
- **Dragging a group moves its members** by one delta, in one entry in the undo
  history. The delta is snapped rather than each member's position: rounding
  them separately moves them by different amounts and shears the group apart.
- The **inspector for a group** lists what is in it, in the diagram's own words,
  and offers the two ways to change it — take one out, or ungroup entirely.

📦 **The boundary tool became a grouping gesture.** Drawing a box around tiles
now means "these belong together": they and the new boundary become a group, and
the rectangle you see afterwards is the derived one, which may snap tighter or
looser on release. A box drawn around nothing has nothing to derive from and
stays exactly where it was drawn — that is the decorative boundary.

🚫 **Every refusal says why.** A gesture that would put a group inside itself,
put two boundaries in one group, or leave a group with no tile in it writes
nothing and says so. A refused gesture that looks identical to a broken one is
the worst of the three outcomes available.

Grouping nests rather than steals: grouping a subset of a group's members puts
the new group _inside_ it. Pulling them out into a sibling would leave the
parent holding only its boundary, with nothing to frame — a document the
editor's own validator rejects. Recorded in
[ADR 0003](/architecture/decisions/adr-0003-a-boundary-is-an-element-a-group-is-a-relation).
